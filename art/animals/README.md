# art/animals: wildlife (family `Animal`)

Decorative wildlife for DEMIURGE: herds, deer, big cats, flocks, whales and fish shoals. These meshes replace the
procedural critter stand-ins in `Unreal/Source/Demiurge/Game/Nature/DmNatureCritters.cpp` for close views. Everything
here is generated by script. Nothing is hand-edited and there are no external inputs (licence: original).

Visual targets: `docs/concept/units/wildlife-herds.png`, `wildlife-birds.png`, `wildlife-sea.png` and the notes in
`docs/concept/units/index.md`.

`manifest.json` has the exact numbers for every mesh: size, triangles per LOD, bones, animations, shape keys, biome
tints, textures, checks and previews.

Files:
- `export/SM_Animal_<Asset>_<Variant>.fbx`: static mesh, an FBX LOD group (`LOD0`…`LOD2/3`), one material slot `Structure`.
  - UV0 is the texture UV.
  - UV1 `AnimWeights` (x) is the per-vertex weight the in-game critter material uses (`DmNatureCritters.h`): head weight for grazers, deer and cats, wing weight for birds, tail weight for fish and whale.
  - Vertex colour is the coat colour, so the existing `M_NatureCreature` (vertex colour times tint) works unchanged.
  - Shape keys sit on LOD0 (on every LOD for the birds). Unreal drops morph targets on a static mesh import, so in the game they live in the `SK_` file (same names, imported as morph targets); for instanced crowds the material's UV1 motion does the flap, tail beat and graze.
- `export/SK_Animal_<Asset>_<Variant>.fbx`: the LOD0 mesh skinned to an armature (root, spine, neck, head, tail, three bones per leg, or wing, hand, fin and tail chains), with every action as an FBX take. All cycles loop in place at 24 fps (no root motion).
- `textures/T_Animal_<Asset><Variant>_{D,N,ORM}.png`: 1024² (512² for birds and fish), baked from a high-poly source of 60k to 270k triangles.
  - D is sRGB.
  - N is tangent space, OpenGL (+Y), so flip green on import, like the rest of the project (`art/tools/dmtex.py`).
  - ORM is linear: R ambient occlusion, G roughness, B metallic (only the fish flanks are metallic).
- `source/<key>.blend`: LODs, the skinned mesh, armature, actions and materials (textures relative); `source/<key>.json` holds the numbers that build the manifest; `source/export-check.json` holds the re-import check.
- Previews: `shots/asset-animals-<key>-{close,40m,300m,1km,anim}.png` and `shots/asset-animals-lineup-close.png`.

## How it is built

`species_animals.py` describes each animal as a skeleton plus primitives tied to bones:
- rounded cones and ellipsoids for the body masses;
- explicit tubes for horns, antlers, beaks, thin tails and bird legs;
- paint-only shapes for eyes and patches.

`build_animals.py` runs these steps:
1. Evaluates the smooth union of the primitives as a signed distance field in numpy, meshes it with OpenVDB (bundled with Blender), drops loose specks, and adds muscle and fur noise (plus a shaggy clump displacement on the lion's mane and the bison's cape). This is the high-poly bake source.
2. Colours the high poly per vertex from species coat functions: belly lines, brindle, rump patches, manes, the whale's white belly and flippers, the fish's silver flanks.
3. Builds LOD0 as a symmetric quadric decimation of the body plus low-sided tubes, cleans out degenerate triangles, then makes smart UVs.
4. Bakes with Cycles, high poly to LOD0: normal, coat colour, roughness, metal and AO.
5. Makes LOD1 to LOD3, each by decimating the previous LOD (UVs carried). For each LOD it tries plain collapse and collapse that protects the extremities (legs, lower legs, head, wing tips, flippers, fluke), with and without X symmetry, and keeps the candidate whose surface stays closest to LOD0 (lost and added parts, mean and 95th percentile) within about 30 % of the triangle target. Walkers and birds stop at LOD2 = 20 % of LOD0, the whale at 12 % plus a 5 % LOD3 hull.
6. Builds the armature from the same skeleton. Skin weights come from the distance to each bone's primitives (top 3 bones per vertex). Animations are procedural keyframes; shape keys are posed meshes.
7. Exports the FBX files (`FBX_SCALE_ALL`, -Z forward, Y up, smoothing groups, tangents, triangulated, sRGB vertex colour, no leaf bones) and saves the `.blend` and the JSON.

`preview_animals.py` renders the preview views in EEVEE:
- Lighting is a warm sun at 20° from the camera's left, with a cool sky fill.
- Every instance uses the LOD that Unreal's screen-size thresholds (1.0 / 0.35 / 0.12 / 0.04) would pick at that distance.
- The far views place a herd, flock or shoal as in the concept art: a migration ribbon, a murmuration over bright shallows, a V of egrets over the delta, a whale and calf, a bait ball.
- A neutral 1.8 m figure stands in the close and 40 m views.

`check_animals.py` re-imports every FBX and checks:
- LOD group, triangles equal to the manifest, UV1, vertex colour, the `Structure` slot, shape keys and size;
- every LOD closed (no open or non-manifold edges beyond a handful), walkers' LOD1 and LOD2 still standing on their legs, birds' and the whale's LOD1 and LOD2 keeping at least 70 % of the span (added by the verification pass);
- bone count and every action in the skeletal file.

## Regenerate

From the project root (every Blender run goes through `tools/render_slot.py`):

```
python art/animals/run_all.py                      # build all, render all previews + lineup, write manifest.json
python art/animals/run_all.py build grazer-bison   # one asset (keys: see run_all.py KEYS)
python art/animals/run_all.py preview all close,40m
python art/animals/run_all.py manifest
python tools/render_slot.py -- "C:/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.2/blender.exe" -b --python-exit-code 1 --python art/animals/check_animals.py
```

A single asset builds in 10 to 20 s, the whole family in about a minute on 4 parallel slot-gated jobs, and previews take a few minutes.

## Integration notes (for the lead's integration agent)

- **Axes:** front is -Y in Blender, as `docs/ASSETS.md` asks; every mesh in this family faces the same way. `docs/ASSETS.md` says that lands on Unreal +X after import, but `docs/lab/asset-pipeline.md` (the shrine, measured in AssetLab) found Blender -Y arriving as Unreal +Y unless the mesh is turned, so **check one import first** (verification note, 2026-11-19). The critter material (`M_NatureCreature`, `Scripts/nature.py`) needs:
  - grazers facing local +X (the graze offset pushes the head +X and down);
  - birds and fish facing local +Y (they fly or swim along a circle whose centre is local -X; the fish tail beat is along local X).
  So whatever the import does, the rotation that makes them match goes **into the mesh** (FBX import rotation, or the mesh transform in the critter factory), **not onto the instance**: the material computes the flight circle and tail beat in local space, so a yawed instance would fly sideways.
- **Pivots:**
  - Walkers: centre of the footprint on the ground.
  - Birds and fish: body centre.
  - Whale: on the sea surface with the back just breaking it (body axis 1.2 m below). For a submerged swim, put the origin about 0.4 m below the surface.
- **Flock scale:** real sizes are modelled. The stand-ins exaggerate birds to about 1.8 m span and fish to 0.75 to 1.15 m so that they read from the god camera, so `manifest.json` gives a `flock_scale` per mesh (starling 3, egret 1.3, fish 3). The previews use it.
- **Materials:** one slot, `Structure`. Author `M_Animal_Coat`: D times tint, ORM, N (flip green), a little sheen tinted by D for fur (about 0.2), specular about 0.3. Make one MI per mesh (`MI_Animal_<Asset><Variant>`, textures) and per biome tint listed in the manifest (`biome_variants`). Wildlife carries no culture colour (`docs/concept/units/index.md`), so there are no culture variants.
- **No states:** animals are not damageable buildings.

## Known gaps

- Fur is a baked normal and colour only. There are no hair cards, so the close-up (under 10 m) silhouettes are smooth. At the game's 20 to 60 px this does not show.
- The skeletal FBX carries LOD0 only. Let Unreal generate skeletal LODs, or use the static LOD group with the material WPO (UV1) for crowds.
- Cycles are generic procedural gaits: a lateral-sequence walk, a graze loop, a flap, a swim. There is no run or gallop, no turning and no root motion.
- Birds and fish are below the 300-tri floor of the person or animal budget on purpose: they are instanced by the hundred or thousand.
- The doe's head still reads slightly long at close range.
- At 1 km a real-size starling is under a pixel, even at flock scale 3 (see `shots/asset-animals-bird-starling-1km.png`). At that zoom the game should draw the murmuration with a larger flock scale (about 6) or as point sprites, as the concept notes suggest (dark points of about 2 px).
- The previews use flat ground and a simple sea. There is no dust haze; the concept's herd dust is an FX job (`DM.FX` dust), not part of the mesh.
- Bake rays can miss on very thin parts (wing tips). The ray distance is widened for birds and fish, but a texel or two at the tips may carry the edge colour.
- The birds' baked normals are kept at a third of their tilt (see the verification section), so close up the wings shade a little faceted. At 2 to 40 px it does not show.
- Grazing muzzles sink 3 to 6 cm below Z = 0 in `HeadDown` and the Graze cycle, and walk-cycle hooves and paws up to 4.5 cm. Ground cover hides it; on bare rock it would show.
- LOD2 of the walkers and birds is 20 % of LOD0, not the ~12 % of `docs/ASSETS.md`: at 12 % the collapse either dropped the legs or webbed them into skirts. That is about 70 extra triangles per animal.

## Verification pass (2026-11-19, independent)

Regenerated from a clean `python art/animals/run_all.py`: the triangle counts per LOD and the sizes of all 11 assets matched the builder's exactly. After the fixes, two further full runs gave the same triangle counts and sizes. Checked against `docs/ASSETS.md` and fixed:

1. **Far LODs had lost their silhouette (fixed).** LOD2 of the walkers had lost their legs where measured: the wildebeest's lowest point was 0.49 m up and the stag's 0.22 m. The birds' LOD2 had no wings (the egret's span was 0.33 m instead of 1.42 m, the starling's 0.09 m instead of 0.39 m), so their wing shape keys and UV1 flap did nothing. The whale's LOD2 and LOD3 had lost the flippers, and LOD3 had lost the fluke (10.6 m long instead of 15.2 m). These are the LODs seen from 300 m to 2 km. The cause was that the quadric collapse flattened thin parts into zero-volume strips, which `clean_degenerate` then deleted. `make_lod` now chains the LODs, protects the extremities and scores candidates by surface distance to LOD0 (`build_animals.py`). Walkers and birds go to 20 % at LOD2 (`species_animals.py` default).
2. **Open edges (fixed).** Every LOD except the fish had 6 to 36 open edges along the x = 0 seam: duplicate edges left by the symmetric collapse. `clean_degenerate` now ends with `Mesh.validate`, and every LOD is closed. Up to 6 edges per mesh are shared by three faces, where tubes meet the body.
3. **Dark facets on the birds' wings (fixed).** 8 % of the starling's and 3 % of the egret's normal texels tilted past 70°, some facing into the surface, so the egret showed a black patch on its left wing. Normals that face inward now become flat, every map is limited to about 70° of tilt, and the birds keep a third of the baked tilt (`write_textures`).
4. **Bait-ball preview (fixed).** The fish pointed radially outward like a starburst; they now mill along the circle as in `wildlife-sea.png` (`preview_animals.py`).
5. **Axis note (corrected).** The note to "yaw birds and fish in the critter factory" was ambiguous, and it assumed Blender -Y lands on Unreal +X. See Integration notes: rotate the mesh, not the instance, and check one import first.
6. **Checks added.** `check_animals.py` now also fails on open edges, on walkers whose LOD1 or LOD2 has lost its legs, and on birds or the whale whose LOD1 or LOD2 has lost its span.
