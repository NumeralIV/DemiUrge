   tale: The Book of Nulias
     In the beginning Nulias was fire and then stone, and the god called ice down out of the dark. Where it fell, the hollows filled, and that was the first sea, and in time it covered the greater part of the world. Then green came out of the wet places, slowly at first, as if it were not sure it was allowed.
     The first people were the Vai. The god woke them in the woods at Nara, and they called the god Thovi. After them came the Findavothi, the Wanori, the Vianiasi, the Wamvisiari, the Nitari and the Thisdi, peoples with their own tongues and their own names for the god.
     In 3,513 years the peoples of Nulias founded 133 settlements, and at their height they numbered 7,338,059 souls. The greatest of their places was Dountha. The furthest of them reached the Age of the Stars.
     They were not always at peace. They fought five wars, in which eleven towns changed hands.
     The god was known for rain, green growth and healing. The scribes wrote more of the god's mercy than of the god's anger. Of the prayers the scribes wrote down, 401 were answered and seven went unheard. In the end, most of them called the god the Cloud-Shepherd.
     There were hard times. Twelve mountains woke in fire, hunger came 254 times, sickness came 31 times and 23 towns were taken by the sea. Of all the places the peoples built, 25 lay in ruins.
     These things the scribes remembered above all. In the first year, before there was counting, the god touched the ground at Nara, and the Vai woke. They looked up at the sky and did not yet know whom to thank, so they thanked everything. In the year 1,371, in the lands about Earan a new people arose, the Wamvisiari, descended from the Vai but no longer their subjects. In the year 2,468, Sal Worirsian drowned, and its bells could be heard under the waves, the sailors said. In the year 3,294, the world turned a page: the Vai, first of all peoples, came into the Age of the Stars, and the others began to hurry.
     In the 3513th year the Vai rose from Nara on a pillar of light. They carried seed, and songs, and a carved stone bearing the name of Thovi, whom they called the Cloud-Shepherd. Before the clouds closed below them, they looked back at Nulias and wept, not from grief, but because they had been loved, and knew it, and were taking that with them too. Wherever they land, there will be a shrine before there is a house.
