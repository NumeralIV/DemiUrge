Summary. 8 ships (ReedBoat, Canoe, Galley, Cog, SailShip, Steamship, ContainerShip, FastFreighter) x 3 states (Intact, Damaged, Ruin) = 24 meshes, one FBX each with LOD0-LOD3 under a LodGroup node (art/ships/export/SM_Ship_<Asset>_A_<State>.fbx). They hold up against docs/ASSETS.md and read well against docs/concept/units at 300 m and 1 km. Four real defects were fixed in the scripts. The ships are still clean, low-detail models in close-up, short of near-photoreal.

Reproducibility. build_ships.py --tex was run from scratch. All 12 trim-sheet PNGs came back byte-identical to the builder's, and the manifest was identical too. After the fixes, two more builds produced identical manifests. check_ships.py: 24/24 OK. All renders went through tools/render_slot.py.

Independent audit (my own script, over all 96 LOD meshes in source/ships.blend):
- Hierarchy: each mesh has a LodGroup root at the origin with an identity transform, and the LODs are parented to it with no offsets.
- Materials and geometry: 3 slots in the fixed order Structure, Roof, Detail (the boats and wrecks leave Roof unused). No degenerate faces and no n-gons.
- UVs: V is inside 0-1 on every LOD. U tiles on the trim sheets, as the README documents.
- Normals: the outward-facing share is 99.1-100 % on every LOD. I traced the remaining hits to rays coming from below the waterline into open container bottoms, plus coplanar transom and end-cap pairs; none is a real flip.
- LOD ratios: LOD1 36-47 %, LOD2 10-18 %, LOD3 6-15 % (130-380 triangles).
- Budget: every ship is 3.1-4.8k, inside the 3-15k ship budget. The reed boat (2.1k) and canoe (1.6k) are boat-sized on purpose.
- Scale: the white 1.8 m reference figure stands in every 40 m view. Crew are now 1.75 m. Bulwarks are 1.0-1.1 m on the big ships, deckhouse storeys are 2.8 m, and containers are 12.2 x 2.44 x 2.6 m. The lengths are plausible: trireme 35 m, cog 29 m, full-rigger 57 m, container feeder 153 m.
- Pivot: the waterline at Z = 0, centred, with the bow toward -Y. The manifest has every required field, plus draft, deck height, sim ages and the towns-kit slot.

Fixes (made in the scripts; recorded in art/ships/README.md, "Independent verification"):
1. Decks: the whole beam was stretched over one band. On every tapering deck quad the planks zig-zagged (an affine UV on a trapezoid) and came out about 1.4 m wide, which is plainly visible on the steamship and sailing-ship close views. Decks are now cut into straight strips parallel to the centreline and planar-mapped. The inner bulwark and end caps share the edge vertices, so there are no T-junctions (open-edge counts are unchanged).
2. Reed boat: the outer lashing rings floated beside the curled bow and stern as black hooks. They now follow the curl.
3. Torn sails and flags: the damage pass dropped random cells, which left loose squares of sail floating in mid-air. Cloth is now kept only when it connects to the yard (or the hoist), and the torn grid is jittered so the holes look ragged rather than square.
4. Crew figures stood at 95 % of their nominal height. Body plus head now equals the requested height.

Open for others:
- The FBX LodGroup has to be imported with Import Mesh LODs on, and that is unconfirmed until the first Unreal import. art/tools/ue_import.py expects the shrine layout (art/export/<asset>/, one FBX per LOD), so it cannot import this family as it is.
- The integration agent has to wire CultureTint (MI_Ship_<Slot>_C0..C5) and the M-mask emissive (night windows in R, engine glow in B).
- The units layer has to exaggerate ship scale. At real scale a galley is about 15 px at 1 km.
- The concept wants light hulls on deep water. The dark wooden and steel hulls carry little contrast there, so a hull rim or brightening parameter in M_Ship_Structure is recommended.
- Missing from the meshes: funnel smoke, wakes, and sail, flag and oar motion.

Art limits behind the 7:
- At 40 m the ships are clean and low in detail: flat painted planes, black-square windows, box castles, and no weathering beyond the trim sheets. They are convincing at game distance but a long way from near-photoreal in close-up.
- Steel wrecks reuse the wood char band, so they read as brown or rusty rather than sooty.
- There is one design per ship (_A).
