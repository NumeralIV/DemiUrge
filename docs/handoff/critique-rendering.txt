LENS: rendering
VERDICT: Not ready for a parallel build from the rendering lens. The individual module APIs are sound (the Frame block is all mat4/vec4, 576 bytes with no
std140 traps; attributes are float-only; depth precision is fine once near/far are pinned; Post needs no depth texture), but the contracts between
render modules are missing: who writes and uploads the Frame block, who binds the shadow map and what a depth program is, how modules see the camera,
quality level and each other's data, and the `world.rivers` shape. Those three blockers plus the Objects re-instancing and budget numbers will each be
built several incompatible ways. The three decisions most likely to make it look cheap are the undetailed 300 px ground triangles and sparse trees at
close zoom, the bloom/exposure calibration that makes the whole day side glow pastel, and the atmosphere split across two authors that will leave a
seam on the planet's edge (with 6 px shadow texels at close zoom a close fourth). All 18 fixes are paste-in text; with them applied the documents are
ready.

[16] MINOR — SPEC-RENDER §6 Ocean, Lakes, rivers, roads, brush ring; §4 step 3.4 ("depth write on")
PROBLEM: Four details will be built wrong as written. (1) "The same triangles on a sphere at DM.R + seaLevel" with per-vertex depth "seaLevel - elev" bakes sea
level into two vertex buffers, so every comet step and every climate change forces a 40,962-vertex rebuild and the sea jumps instead of rising. (2)
"Lakes at elev + water" per vertex makes boundary triangles slope from the lake level down onto the dry neighbour's ground, coplanar with the terrain
there: z-fighting around every lake. (3) River and road polylines run cell to neighbour, i.e. exactly along mesh edges where the two adjacent
triangles slope differently; "slightly above the ground" with depth write on gives ribbons half buried and half floating, in 60 deg zigzags. (4) The
brush ring is drawn only by the terrain shader, so it disappears under deep water, exactly when the player raises land from the sea or aims a comet.
FIX: §6: "Ocean vertices are sea-level independent: aPos = unit direction, aData = (elev, sea ice, 0, 0). The vertex shader places the vertex at radius
`uPlanet.x + uPlanet.y` (plus waves) and computes depth = `uPlanet.y - aData.x`. The renderer eases `uPlanet.y` toward `world.seaLevel` at about 1
unit per second. The ocean index buffer may be all of `geo.tris`: it is drawn after the terrain, so land triangles are depth-rejected, and with no sea
(level -9) the sphere is entirely underground. Lakes: emit every triangle that has at least one lake vertex, with all three corners at that lake's
surface radius, so the shore is a depth-buffer intersection like the ocean's. Rivers and roads: smooth the cell polyline (two Chaikin passes), sample
it every 0.5 units or less, put each ribbon vertex at `groundAt + 0.03`, draw with polygonOffset(-2, -2) and depth write OFF. Only the ocean writes
depth in drawWater. The brush ring is drawn by both the terrain and the water shaders."

[17] MINOR — SPEC-RENDER §1 `GL.program`, `GL.target`, `GL.mesh`, `GL.instances`, `GL.caps`; §5 Post (HDR fallback)
PROBLEM: Small WebGL2 traps that only the gl.js author can fix for everyone. The default `sampler2D` precision is lowp; on mobile GPUs that honour it, HDR
texture reads (scene resolve, bloom chain) are clamped to about +/-2. RGBA16F is renderable and multisample-able only after `EXT_color_buffer_float`
or `EXT_color_buffer_half_float` is enabled, and its sample limit is not MAX_SAMPLES. A multisampled colour buffer needs a depth renderbuffer with the
same sample count or the FBO is incomplete. A multisample resolve blit needs identical formats and identical rectangles and cannot target the canvas.
`uniformBlockBinding` on a program that uses no Frame member (blur passes) gets INVALID_INDEX. `mesh` cannot draw a sub-range or grow, but rivers,
roads and bolts change size. The terrain needs more than one vec4 of per-vertex data. The RGBA8 fallback clamps "sun-lit white 2.5" to pure white
everywhere. Checking link status inside `GL.program` forces serial shader compilation.
FIX: Add to §1: "The prepended header also contains `precision highp sampler2D; precision highp samplerCube;`. `caps.hdr`: enable EXT_color_buffer_float,
else EXT_color_buffer_half_float, then verify with a 4x4 RGBA16F framebuffer. `caps.maxSamples` = min(4, largest value of
`getInternalformatParameter(RENDERBUFFER, <colour format>, SAMPLES)`). `GL.target` with samples > 0 allocates colour and depth renderbuffers with the
same sample count; `depthTex` with samples > 0 throws. `blitTo(dst)`: dst is single-sample with the same size and colour format, never the canvas. The
Frame binding is skipped when the block index is INVALID_INDEX. `mesh.draw(mode = TRIANGLES, count = mesh.count, first = 0)`; `mesh.update` and
`mesh.setIndex` reallocate when given a larger array and update `mesh.count`; `inst.upload(first = 0, count = inst.count)`. `GL.mesh` also accepts
`aI0 ... aI3` as extra per-vertex vec4 attributes for meshes that are never instanced. Link status is checked on the first `prog.use()`, not inside
`GL.program`." Add to §5: "When `!caps.hdr` the renderer sets `uSunDir.w = 0.4` (`shade` scales sun, ambient and emissive by it), and Post divides
exposure by 0.4 and skips bloom."

[18] MINOR — SPEC-RENDER §6 Terrain ("face normal from screen-space derivatives")
PROBLEM: Derivative normals are the right call here: an indexed mesh of 40,962 vertices cannot give 81,920 triangles a unique provoking vertex each, and `flat`
varyings hit ANGLE's slow provoking-vertex path on D3D11 (Windows). But three rules are missing. If the world-position varying is declared `centroid`,
the derivatives are garbage along every triangle edge under MSAA. From orbit a triangle is about 10 px and at the limb it is sub-pixel; the 2x2
derivative quad then straddles several triangles and the normal is noise, which shows as sparkling snow, flickering specular and a shimmering
terminator. And nothing says the ocean must not use them.
FIX: Add to §6: "The world-position varying is plain `smooth` (never `centroid`). No shader in the project uses `flat` varyings. Facet weight = 0.85 x
smoothstep(2.0, 8.0, 1.9 / length(fwidth(worldPos))): fully faceted when a cell edge spans 8 px or more, pure smooth vertex normal below 2 px and
toward the limb. The ocean, lakes and cloud shell use the analytic sphere normal plus procedural waves, never derivative normals."
