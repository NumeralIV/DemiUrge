  --- tale: The Chronicle of Erethe ---
  In the beginning Erethe was fire. The god cooled it and called down the ice of heaven, until the sea covered the greater part of its face. Then green came upon the land, and beasts, and the god looked for a people to wake.

  The first people were the Umari. The god woke them in the woods at Vornhal, and they called the god Ashvel. After them came the Teshki, the Orun, the Vaeli and the Hsar, peoples with their own tongues and their own names for the god.

  In 2,000 years the peoples of Erethe founded 60 settlements, and at their height they numbered 3,455,327 souls. The greatest of their places was Wenmarrow. The furthest of them reached the Age of the Stars.

  They were not always at peace. They fought ten wars, in which twelve towns changed hands.

  The god was known for rain, healing and falling stars. The scribes wrote more of the god's mercy than of the god's anger. Of the prayers the scribes wrote down, 24 were answered and five went unheard. In the end, most of them called the god the Well-Filler.

  There were hard times. Five stars fell, two mountains woke in fire, hunger came 16 times, sickness came 15 times and six towns were taken by the sea. Of all the places the peoples built, 16 lay in ruins.

  These things the scribes remembered above all. In the first year, before there was counting, the god touched the ground at Vornhal, and the Umari woke. They looked up at the sky and did not yet know whom to thank, so they thanked everything. In the year 900, from the Umari came the Vaeli, as a branch comes from a tree: the same wood, pointing elsewhere. In the year 1,400, the people of the far valleys about Oduya spoke the old tongue so strangely that the capital laughed at them. In that year they stopped coming to be laughed at, and called themselves the Hsar. In the year 1,923, the Hsar turned their eyes to the stars, and built towers of fire to reach them. They were the first in all the world.

  In the 2000th year the Umari went up from Vornhal to the stars. They were not sure, at the end, what Ashvel had been to them: a parent, a storm, a story. So they did what grown children do: they said a careful goodbye, and promised to write, and did not say when. Somewhere above the clouds one of them looked back and raised a hand, just in case.
